# The zoom sweep — every screen at 1280×720, across the range

The last ticket on [FlowMap zoom](https://github.com/matheus-sancha/FlowMap/issues/46) (#52). Its job
was **not** to make the app fit — five tickets had already done that — but to find out where the
claim the whole map rests on turns out to be false.

That claim: the tree is told the window is `size / scale`, so every hardcoded pixel multiplies for
free and **the sweep is verification rather than a rewrite**. If that were wrong, this sheet would be
long.

## What was driven

Debug build at `663a2d8` + the control, in a window pinned to **1280×720** — a 14" laptop at
Windows' default 150 % scaling, which is the screen this whole effort is designed against.

**Both extremes, because the middle cannot fail on its own.** At 70 % the tree sees 1829×1029, which
is *more* room than 100 % gives; nothing that holds at 100 % can break by being given more. At 150 %
it sees **853×480 — below the 1100×700 this app has always declared as its minimum.** That is the
interesting end, and it is reachable on purpose: §12.10's floor shrinks with the scale but never
grows, so zooming in on a small window is allowed to show you less.

**Overflow was read from the console, not from screenshots.** A debug build prints
`RenderFlex overflowed by N pixels` the moment a box does not fit, which catches what an eye passes
over — and says which direction and by how much.

Walked: Projects · Study Templates (via the rail) · Settings · About · a study's Flow, Study
Settings, Capacity, Demand and Summary · the mode switch · Simulation with a live run · Simulation
Overview, Production Plan, Production Gantt, Occupation, Delivery Float · Compare · the run banner ·
the scale menu itself.

A run was made during the sitting (`On-time delivery: 100 %`, 60 of 60 orders, bottleneck END at
38 %), because four of the five results tabs render nothing without one.

### A — Occupation overflows by 47 px at 150 %

`occupation_view.dart`'s pane is a `Column` of: a `Wrap` of segmented buttons
(*Per Workcenter · Per Line · Per Type*, then *% · Hours · Gap*, then *Month*), a legend, and an
`Expanded` holding the chart-and-grid.

At 150 % the tree has ~150 logical pixels of body height here. The `Wrap` wraps onto a second run and
the header alone needs ~197, so `Expanded` is asked for negative space and the `Column` overflows by
the difference.

**Not fixed in this ticket, deliberately.** The fix is a design choice — pin the header and scroll
the whole pane, or let the header scroll away with the grid — on a screen whose layout §12 records
two previous rounds of argument about (#16, #13). It is
[Occupation's header squeezes its grid to nothing at 150 %](https://github.com/matheus-sancha/FlowMap/issues/54).

Worth keeping in proportion: in a release build an overflow is silent clipping rather than a striped
banner, and it is reachable only by zooming *in* on a window already at the app's floor.

### B — `_PaceSetterField` throws, and has nothing to do with the scale

`study_settings_tab.dart:252` builds a `DropdownButtonFormField` with
`initialValue: study.paceSetterTargetId` and items from `flowTargetsProvider` — the targets reachable
from *this study's flow*. The live study carries `fe78357b-…`, which is not among them, so the
dropdown's "exactly one item with this value" assertion fires on every build of the tab.

A dangling pacemaker reference: the target was chosen, and the step pointing at it later left the
flow. **Data-dependent and viewport-independent** — it reproduces at every scale, and it is not a fit
defect. Raised separately rather than on the zoom map.

## What this settled

**The claim held.** Seventeen checks across two extremes and every screen in the app produced **one**
layout failure, on one screen, at one end of the range, in a window below the app's own stated
minimum. Nothing needed rewriting; `simulation_tab.dart`'s `maxWidth < 1100` branch,
`result_table.dart`'s 110–150 column widths and `gantt_view.dart`'s `_labelWidth` were never touched
and never misbehaved, because none of them could tell anything had changed.

**Clipping is not breakage, and the app was already built for it.** Three separate rows run out of
room at 150 % — the filter bar and both tab strips — and all three scroll, because they were made
scrollable long before there was a zoom. That is the difference between a control that is off screen
and one that is gone.

**70 % is the answer to the complaint that opened the map.** The Occupation view at 100 % in this
window shows a filter bar that runs off the right edge and a grid you read a few columns at a time.
At 70 % the same window holds all seven filters, the date slicer, both period pickers, the chart and
fifteen month columns plus TOTAL — at once.

## Still owed

- **#54**, above.
- **The pacemaker dropdown**, above — not this map's.
- **Light mode.** Every screen in this sitting was dark. The scale is a geometry change and cannot
  care about a palette, which is the argument for not re-walking it — but it is an argument, not an
  observation, and `DRIVE-2026-09-07.md` records light mode escaping four sittings in a row on
  exactly that kind of reasoning.
- **Portuguese and Spanish.** The rail's width is set by its longest destination label, so a longer
  locale makes the rail wider and the body narrower. Nothing here was driven in anything but English.
- **The dialogs.** New project, duplicate study, import and export were not opened at either extreme.
  They carry their own sizes and do not inherit a screen's constraints, which is exactly the shape of
  thing this sheet was looking for.
