## D — Occupation, phase 5 (`DRIVE-occupation.md`)

The suite covers the properties: a structural filter never shrinks what a machine was asked for, an
order-level filter moves the share and not the band, the PLANT row vanishes the moment the station
set narrows, and an aggregate can never be worse than its worst member — the arithmetic that killed
the chart. **What it cannot cover is whether the grid is readable.**

Only **6 of 150** runs can draw this view; the other 144 predate v25's monthly capacity. Pick a
recent run.

13. **`Chart | Grid` switch** — restored in `795ac6e` after the field asked for the chart to be
    *adjusted*, not deleted. **Grid is the default.** Flip both ways; on the chart, confirm the
    stack now reads from `OccupationRamp` rather than the generated Material roles v1.0 used.
14. **Both groupings** — *Per workcenter* and *Per line*.
15. **All three units** — `%`, *Hours* (`733/499`), *Gap* (`-234`). The numbers must stay legible
    inside a month column at each.
16. **The PLANT row** shows only while nothing has narrowed the station set. Apply any structural
    filter and watch it go.
17. **Structural vs order-level, the distinction the whole ticket turns on.** A **structural**
    filter (studies, cells, lines, type, workcenter) moves demand *and* capacity together. An
    **order-level** one (projects, parts, order numbers) leaves capacity fixed and **dims the
    remainder** — the thing that tells *"CEU27 is 100 % and 80 of it is yours"* from *"CEU32 is
    147 % and none of it is"*. Confirm you can read that difference off the cell.
18. **Row height** — 34 pt → 48 pt in `795ac6e`, for a two-line station header. Does a long station
    name now fit?
19. **The two promoted station pickers** (type, workcenter) are in the **shared filter bar**, and
    the **Gantt** and **Overview** obey them too, because their rows are stations.
20. **Non-default thresholds.** *Project settings* → the Occupation pair beside the float
    thresholds; change **85 / 100** to something like **70 / 90** (commits on blur). Return to the
    grid: the bands must move. Then try a **red below the amber** — it should be *refused*, not
    stored.

## Findings

**The sitting did not walk the 23 checks.** It came back with four adjustments instead, which is a
real result and not the sheet's — so **most of what phases 3, 4 and 5 owe is still owed.** What is
recorded below is what the field said, verbatim, and nothing is inferred about the rest.

Reported, in the field's own words:

> - we need to work again on the ocupation chart
> - Clean the app, there is too much explanation and random text
> - The PLANT Line change to total line in the bottom
> - ocupation graph must have the ocupation & as data label. And Y axis in hours to be present

Read as four findings:

1. **The Occupation chart needs another round**, and — asked directly — **it is the default view
   again**. That partly reverses #9, whose argument for the grid was that the aggregate bar has
   never once broken its capacity line (peak 87 %) while single stations hit 149 %. The switch
   `795ac6e` added stays; which side it opens on flips.
2. **Too much explanation and random text.** Asked where it is worst, the answer was **the long
   inline help paragraphs** — not the field-level helpers, not the empty states, not the captions.
   The measurable body: 589 ARB keys, **81 named `*Help`**, 97 strings over 70 characters, and
   about **20 rendered as inline `Text(`** rather than behind a hover. The longest is
   `simGanttGapHelp` at 352 characters, painted on the Gantt every visit.
3. **The PLANT row becomes a TOTAL row, at the bottom.** It is currently `PeriodMatrix.pinned`,
   documented and drawn *at the top*, and the **float matrix uses the same slot** — so this reaches
   a shared widget and has to answer for both.
4. **The chart owes a data label and a Y axis.** `_OccupationPainter` today draws stacked bars, a
   stepped capacity line, month labels and an over-count badge — **there is no Y axis at all**, no
   scale and no data label. So this adds two things that were never there rather than fixing two
   that are wrong.
